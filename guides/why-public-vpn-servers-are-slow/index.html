Performance

Why public VPN servers may be slow.

Public VPN servers are useful, but they do not always behave like paid private infrastructure. Speed and reliability can change quickly.

Too many users on one server

A public VPN server can become slow when many people connect at the same time. Bandwidth is shared, so one popular server may feel fast in the morning and slow later in the day.

Long physical distance

If you connect to a server far away from your location, your traffic has to travel farther. This can increase latency and make websites, video, and downloads feel slower.

Volunteer server limits

Many public VPN servers are operated by volunteers or third parties. They may have limited upload speed, unstable home networks, scheduled downtime, or firewall restrictions.

Server list freshness

A server list can show a server that was recently available but is now overloaded or offline. Refreshing the list or choosing another server can help.

What you can do

Try another country, choose a server with better speed or lower ping, avoid overloaded entries, and reconnect if the tunnel becomes unstable.
